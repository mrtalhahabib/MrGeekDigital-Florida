Does Your Naples Listing Page Show Up in ChatGPT? An AI Search Checklist for Florida Realtors

On this page

A Naples buyer returning from a season of open houses types a question into ChatGPT: “Who is the best realtor in Naples for Pelican Bay condos?” The answer is about three sentences long and names one, maybe two, agents. There is no page two. No map pack to scroll. If you’re not in the answer, you don’t exist — and unlike Google, you can’t easily check where you stand. This checklist fixes that.

How AI Tools Actually Pick a Name

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google’s AI Overviews don’t rank websites — they synitize answers from sources they trust: review aggregators, local publications, structured data and consistent entity information. The name that appears is the name AI can verify is (a) a real estate agent, (b) in that market, (c) well-reviewed, and (d) mentioned by sources besides itself.

The 9-Point AI Visibility Checklist

Your entity (points 1–3)

  • 1. One name, spelled one way, everywhere. “Jane Smith, REALTOR®” on Google, “Jane Smith P.A.” on Zillow, “Jane Smith Realty” on your broker site — three names means a fragmented entity, and fragmented entities don’t get recommended. Pick your canonical name and enforce it.
  • 2. Your schema says what you are. RealEstateAgent markup with your areaServed (Naples, Pelican Bay, Collier County) on your site. AI reads schema literally.
  • 3. Your Google Business Profile is complete and reviewed. GBP is a primary fact source for local answers. Wrong categories or a stale profile quietly disqualify you.

Your proof (points 4–6)

  • 4. Reviews that name the neighborhood. “Helped us buy in Pelican Bay” teaches both Google and LLMs where you win. Coach the mention naturally at closings.
  • 5. Community pages with citable facts. HOA ranges, membership costs, beach-access details — the specific numbers AI quotes when answering “what about Pelican Bay?” Vague pages don’t get cited.
  • 6. Local press mentions. One Naples Daily News quote outweighs fifty directory links. AI models weight independent sources heavily.

Your tracking (points 7–9)

  • 7. Baseline the answers now. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews your ten money queries. Screenshot. Date them. You can’t improve what you haven’t measured.
  • 8. Re-check monthly. AI answers shift faster than rankings. Track share-of-answer, not positions.
  • 9. Ask the question the way buyers do. Not “best realtor Naples” — “who should I call to buy a condo in Pelican Bay?” Long, specific, conversational. That’s where this is all heading.
  • Bonus — watch the follow-up questions. When an AI tool answers and the buyer types “how do I contact them,” the entity that wins is the one whose phone, website and reviews are consistent enough to surface instantly. Consistency isn’t glamorous. It’s the whole game.

How Long Before You Show Up?

Entity fixes show effects in AI answers within 60–90 days — faster than classic SEO because you’re correcting facts, not earning authority. But here’s the strategic point most agents miss: AI models reinforce whoever they already recommend. Every month you wait, a competitor’s name gets a little more default. What AI Search Is Not (Debunking Three Things)

“It’s just a fad.” The behavior shift is already measurable: buyers ask conversational questions and expect a named recommendation. Google embedding AI Overviews into results is not a beta — it’s the default for a growing share of queries, including “best realtor in [city].”

“You can pay to be the answer.” You can’t. AI recommendations are synthesized, not auctioned. The only inputs that matter — consistent entity, strong reviews, citable content, independent mentions — are earned, which is precisely why they’re worth having.

“It replaces your website.” AI answers need sources. Your community pages, market data and structured profile are what get quoted. Agents who abandon their sites for AI-chasing have it exactly backward: the site is the food the AI eats.

The Ten-Minute Test

Before you close this tab: open ChatGPT and Gemini, and ask each one, the way a real buyer would — “who’s the best realtor in [your city] for [your specialty]?” Take screenshots. If you appear, ask three more variations and see how fragile it is. If you don’t, you’ve just created your baseline — and the checklist above is your twelve-month project list. Either way, you now know something most agents in your market have never checked.

Key Takeaways

  • AI answers name one or two agents — there is no page two to hide on.
  • Fix the entity first: one name, RealEstateAgent schema, complete GBP.
  • Reviews and community pages that name the neighborhood are what LLMs quote.
  • Baseline your 10 money queries now; AI reinforces whoever it already recommends.
